SiC substrate market recovery: what it means for power packaging

Key takeaways

A research summary from September 2026 reports that the SiC substrate market is showing signs of recovery on EV, renewable energy and data center power demand. The pace of price recovery, the move to 8-inch wafers and package requirements are the open questions.

A research summary from September 2026 reports that the SiC substrate market is showing signs of recovery after a period of falling prices. Silicon carbide (SiC) substrates are the wafers on which SiC power devices are built. Their supply and price influence the cost and availability of devices used in EV inverters, renewable energy converters and data center power.

What is driving the SiC substrate market recovery?

The summary attributes the recovery to a gradually recovering EV market, expanding renewable energy deployment and planned high-voltage direct current (HVDC) power architectures for AI data centers.

Demand grew after SiC power modules entered EV traction inverters in 2018. Suppliers added capacity aggressively, and when EV momentum weakened, substrate prices dropped sharply. The summary estimates that EVs account for 65% of SiC device applications in 2026, the largest segment.

A March 2026 analysis describes AI data centers as a second growth engine for SiC, consistent with the HVDC point. The same analysis reports that AI server power designs are planned to move from 400V to 800V architectures, and that substrate-only suppliers have faced more earnings pressure than vertically integrated device makers.

Where the practical constraints sit

A substrate price recovery reaches device buyers only after the wafers pass through epitaxy, device fabrication, qualification and packaging. The public summary gives no price or capacity figures, and its chart list refers to a six-inch price rebound expected in 2027 without showing values.

Wafer format is a second constraint. An 8-inch (200 mm) wafer yields about 1.9 times as many die as a 6-inch wafer and can cut cost by more than 30%, according to an August 2026 industry report. The same report notes that slicing, polishing, epitaxy and ion implantation each add yield risk, so larger wafers save money only if defect rates stay controlled. The March 2026 analysis says yield ramp speed and unit cost control will shape market share, and transition periods can carry underutilization costs.

What we're watching

These open questions will show how the recovery develops and how quickly it reaches device supply:

  • When does the 6-inch price rebound that the report's chart list places in 2027 appear in SiC device pricing, and which voltage classes and customer segments see it first?
  • How quickly do 8-inch lines reach yields that deliver the expected lower unit cost, and how long does qualification take for devices made on them?
  • Does the EV recovery sustain the 65% application share, or do data center programs take a larger share of substrate demand as HVDC designs reach production?
  • What do higher-power SiC modules require from the package in terms of thermal path, interconnect and switching behavior?

Why it matters for manufacturing buyers

The SiC substrate market affects power-module programs through device availability and cost, and package planning can proceed before pricing settles. Hana's OSAT services include power packages. The data center and automotive markets both draw on the same device supply. Where a program dual-sources from multiple countries within the same company, each site is qualified separately against the customer's requirements.
