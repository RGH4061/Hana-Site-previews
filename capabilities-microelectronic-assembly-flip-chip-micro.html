Microelectronic assembly · Flip Chip

Precision flip chip

Precision flip-chip assembly for optical, RF, sensor and other devices where die alignment and interconnect length affect electrical or optical performance. Bumped die are placed face-down and bonded using controlled processes, with X-ray and alignment verification supporting qualification and production.

Flip-chip bump inspection with the bump map on the operator display

Capability overview

The process covers die placement and bonding, underfill and protection, and non-destructive verification of joint and alignment integrity.

Flip chip bonding capabilities

Mounting methods

Contacted connections

Adhesive-based processes. The bump is pressed against the pad and held there by the cured adhesive.

GoldAdhesiveConductive pastePad

ACF / ACP

Anisotropic conductive film / paste

Conductive particles trapped between bump and pad carry current vertically only. The adhesive bonds and insulates everything else.

MachineUnitek MC

NCF / NCP

Non-conductive film / paste

The bump touches the pad directly. As the adhesive cures it shrinks, holding the contact under pressure.

MachineUnitek MC

SBB

Stud bump bonding

Gold stud bumps are dipped in conductive paste, placed onto the pads, then underfilled for strength.

MachineUniversal MC

Metal joints

Bump and pad fuse into one metallurgical joint. Gold-to-gold interconnect is our featured flip chip capability.

GoldSolder / tinEpoxyPad

C4

Controlled collapse chip connection

Solder bumps melt in reflow, collapse onto the pads and self-align, forming a solder-to-solder joint.

MachineUniversal MC

ESC

Epoxy-encapsulated solder connection

Gold bumps join solder or tin on the pad. Epoxy encapsulates and protects the joint.

MachineUniversal MC

GGI

Gold-to-gold interconnect

Gold bumps bond directly to gold pads by thermo-compression or ultrasonic energy. No solder, no adhesive.

MachineTDK AFM-1503

Gold-to-gold flip chip bonding

How we make a GGI joint with an ultrasonic bonding head, in four steps.

Bump and clean

Step 01

Gold bumps are formed on the IC. Plasma cleans the PCB and/or IC bond surfaces.

Contact and load

Step 02

The bond tool holds the flipped IC and brings its bumps onto the pads under load.

Ultrasonic bond

Step 03

With the load held, ultrasonic power scrubs the interface and fuses gold to gold.

Bonded flip chip

Step 04

The tool lifts away, leaving a finished GGI flip chip with no solder or adhesive.

Frequently asked questions

How is precision flip chip different from standard flip chip?+
The interconnect principle is the same (bumped die bonded face-down), but placement accuracy and alignment requirements are tighter for optical, RF and sensor devices where these parameters affect performance.
Which flip-chip bonding methods do you offer?+
Contacted connections (ACF / ACP, NCF / NCP and SBB stud bump bonding) and metal joints (C4, ESC and GGI). Gold-to-gold interconnect (GGI) is our featured process: gold bumps bond directly to gold pads by thermo-compression or ultrasonic energy, with no solder or adhesive.
Which substrates can you bond to?+
Ceramic, glass, PCB, lead frame and other substrates, from 40 × 40 mm up to 170 × 105 mm.
What die sizes and wafers does the GGI bonder handle?+
The TDK AFM-1503 handles die from 0.8 to 3.0 mm with the standard camera, down to 0.3 × 0.3 × 0.1 mm as an option. Wafer supply is 6 inches and maximum bond load is 25 N.
What placement accuracy and throughput can you achieve?+
Mounting accuracy is 7 µm in X and Y and 0.3° in theta. Mounting time is 0.8 sec per shot for a standard chip and 1.6 sec for an H-Package, with capacity of around 1.2 Mpcs per month.
How is the assembly verified?+
Verification includes 2D and 3D (CT) X-ray, placement-accuracy checks and IPC / AEC-Q reliability testing, supported by per-unit traceability.
What quality systems apply?+
ISO 9001 applies group-wide, with IATF 16949 for automotive work and ISO 13485 for medical programs, in ESD-controlled areas meeting ANSI/ESD S20.20.
